## Abstract

This presentation discusses the use of deep generative models for calibrating health decision models. Microsimulation and other health decision models are often used to answer policy questions that cannot be studied directly through clinical studies alone, but these models must be calibrated so their outputs match observed targets such as survival, prevalence, incidence, or surveillance counts. Bayesian calibration is well suited for this task, but it can be computationally expensive because it may require thousands of model runs and often depends on likelihood functions. This talk motivates the use of deep generative approaches as a way to reduce the computational burden and support more flexible calibration workflows when new data become available.
